# cs-330-computer-graphics-and-visualization-

**How do I approach designing software?**

My approach to designing software has become much more structured through this project. I tend to start with a general concept and then break it down into smaller, manageable pieces. For this scene, I began with the idea of a campfire environment and then identified the individual objects that would make it feel complete, such as chairs, a tent, a river, and environmental elements.

**What new design skills has your work on the project helped you to craft?**

One of the biggest skills I developed was spatial thinking in a 3D environment. Designing in 3D required me to think about how objects relate to each other in space, not just individually. I also improved my ability to build more complex objects by combining simple primitives, which is something I had not done extensively before.

**What design process did you follow for your project work?**

My design process for each assignment as well as the project was an iterative process. I started with basic shapes and gradually added more detail over time and adjusted the shapes I had to make the objects appear more like their real-life counterparts. I focused on getting objects placed correctly first, then added textures, then lighting, and finally environmental elements. Each stage built on the previous one.

**How could tactics from your design approach be applied in future work?**

Breaking a large problem into smaller pieces and building incrementally is something I can apply to any future project. The ability to prototype quickly and refine over time is especially useful in both software development and design-heavy work such as database structures, medical implants, gaming, etc.

**How do I approach developing programs?**

When developing programs, I focus on getting a working baseline first and then improving it step by step. I try not to overcomplicate things early on, and instead build functionality in layers as I have a tendancy to overthink problems if I tackle them without that process in place.

**What new development strategies did you use while working on your 3D scene?**

I had attempted to work on creating modular functions and reusable code. Functions for transformations, textures, and materials helped keep the code organized and easier to manage. I also spent more time debugging visually, which was new compared to other programming projects I have had in the past.

**How did iteration factor into your development?**

Iteration was a major part of the process. Many features, especially object placement and camera behavior, required repeated adjustments. Small changes in position or rotation often had a large impact, so I had to test frequently and refine gradually.

**How has your approach to developing code evolved throughout the milestones?**

Earlier in the course, I focused more on just making things work. By the end of the project, I was more focused on structure, readability, and reusability. I also became more comfortable troubleshooting issues related to rendering and spatial relationships.

**How can computer science help me in reaching my goals?**

Computer science provides the foundation for solving complex problems and building systems that can scale. The skills developed in this project, such as problem-solving, debugging, and working with structured code, are directly applicable to my long-term goals in software development.

**How do computational graphics and visualizations apply to your future education?**

This project gave me a better understanding of how visual data can be represented and manipulated. These concepts are useful in areas like simulation, data visualization, and any field that requires interpreting complex systems visually.

**How do computational graphics and visualizations apply to your future professional pathway?**

From a professional perspective, these skills can apply to game development, simulation systems, and visualization tools. I am especially interested in how these concepts could be used in more technical fields, such as biomedical engineering visualization or interactive medical simulations, where understanding spatial relationships in internal implants and medicine is important.
